# Problem Statement

## Background

Ports are complex operational environments where multiple vessels, berths, cranes, and container-handling activities must be coordinated continuously. As vessel arrivals and container volumes fluctuate, high berth utilisation and increasing waiting times can create congestion that affects the flow of port operations.

Port operations teams need to anticipate congestion early and coordinate available resources before a hotspot becomes a major operational bottleneck.

## The Problem

Port operators often need to make time-sensitive decisions about where vessels should berth, which cranes should be assigned, and what operational actions should be prioritised when congestion is expected.

Without an integrated predictive decision-support system, it can be difficult to identify upcoming congestion, understand the operational factors causing it, and determine the best combination of berth, crane, and scheduling actions for the next several hours.

The challenge is therefore to predict container congestion from available operational conditions and turn that prediction into practical recommendations that can help reduce waiting time and improve utilisation of port resources.

## Who Is Affected

The primary users are **port operations managers, shift supervisors, and dispatchers** responsible for coordinating vessel arrivals, berth assignments, crane resources, and short-term operational schedules.

The problem also indirectly affects vessel operators, terminal staff, logistics teams, and other stakeholders when congestion causes increased waiting times or inefficient resource utilisation.

## Why It Matters

Unanticipated congestion can lead to:

- Increased vessel waiting and turnaround times
- Higher pressure on already heavily utilised berths
- Inefficient allocation of cranes and other operational resources
- Delays in container-handling activities
- More difficult short-term scheduling and operational decision-making

Early identification of congestion gives operations teams an opportunity to reassign vessels, adjust resources, and prepare an operational response before congestion becomes critical.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Traditional port operations rely heavily on operational dashboards, schedules, historical information, and the experience of operations teams to make short-term decisions. These tools can show the current state of the port, but they may not provide a unified workflow that connects **future congestion prediction, explanation, resource optimisation, and recommended actions**.

A prediction alone is also insufficient. Operations teams need to understand **why** congestion is expected and **what they should do about it**.

SmartPort AI addresses this gap by connecting congestion prediction with explainable insights, berth and crane optimisation, what-if analysis, and a 72-hour operational action plan in a single decision-support workflow.
